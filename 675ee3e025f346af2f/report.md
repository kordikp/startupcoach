**Founder:** @lauritom  ·  **Project:** startup — https://gitlab.fit.cvut.cz/lauritom/startup

> How can a platform unify ML training tracking, model versioning, and deployment to customer-owned compute infrastructure, addressing the gap where existing experiment trackers stop at the model registry?

## 📊 Market intelligence

Two things this survey told you in August do not survive a second look, and three things have changed since. Correction one: "nobody covers tracking, registry and serving on the customer's own machine without Kubernetes" is false. ClearML's open-source server installs with docker-compose on a single Linux host [[1]](https://clear.ml/docs/latest/docs/deploying_clearml/clearml_server_linux_mac/) and its serving component runs the same way [[2]](https://github.com/clearml/clearml-serving); a Valohai on-premises worker is a systemd service on an Ubuntu machine, with no cluster [[3]](https://docs.valohai.com/installation-and-setup/on-premises/linux-workers); and MLflow has done all three on one machine for years [[4]](https://mlflow.org/docs/latest/ml/deployment/deploy-model-locally/). Correction two: "MLflow has no access control" — the row in your own comparison table — has been out of date since May, when MLflow 3.13 shipped roles, workspace-scoped permissions and an admin interface for a self-hosted server [[5]](https://mlflow.org/releases/3.13.0/). What changed: on 30 September Weights & Biases became a product inside CoreWeave Forge, and its SaaS users now sign in through a GPU cloud's console [[6]](https://www.coreweave.com/companies/weights-and-biases); TrueFoundry replaced the $499 and $2,999 plans quoted in August with per-user pricing for an LLM gateway [[7]](https://www.truefoundry.com/pricing); and Neptune's users have had eight months to settle, with eight destinations listed on the official transition page — one of them a tracker launched for the occasion — and MolaBoard not among them [[8]](https://docs.neptune.ai/transition_hub). So the gap is not architectural. It is commercial: a team of five to twenty people cannot buy a supported self-hosted install from anybody without a sales call. ClearML sells on-premises only in its Enterprise tier [[9]](https://clear.ml/pricing), Comet likewise [[10]](https://www.comet.com/site/pricing/), Valohai quotes every customer individually [[11]](https://valohai.com/pricing/), and the self-serve plan of W&B is SaaS and limited to companies with fewer than 50 employees [[12]](https://coreweave.com/forge-pricing). That is ground MolaBoard can hold — if it ships, and if the price is on the website. Regulatory citations and revenue figures carried over from the August survey were link-checked for this version, not re-read.

**Why now:** The strongest new argument is dated 30 September 2026. CoreWeave launched Forge, which "unifies Weights & Biases Models" with its own training, inference, registry and sandbox services [[13]](https://coreweave.com/news/coreweave-forge-launches-turning-the-ai-loop-production-run-into-a-better-model-and-agent); the W&B pricing page now redirects to coreweave.com, and Free, Pro and Enterprise SaaS accounts sign in through Forge [[6]](https://www.coreweave.com/companies/weights-and-biases). The most widely used commercial tracker is now a feature of a compute vendor. Self-hosted and dedicated instances are unchanged and nothing was shut down, so this is a neutrality argument and not an emergency — and usage agrees: wandb still draws 14.5 million PyPI downloads a month against MLflow's 19.9 million [[14]](https://pypistats.org/packages/mlflow). Second, workloads do keep moving to owned compute. Broadcom's Private Cloud Outlook 2026 (1,800 IT decision-makers, fielded February–March) reports 56% running or planning production AI inference on private cloud and public cloud down 15 points to 41% [[15]](https://investors.broadcom.com/news-releases/news-release-details/broadcoms-private-cloud-outlook-2026-reveals-ai-tipping-point). It is a vendor survey of companies with a thousand employees or more, which is not your buyer. Third, the window that looked best in August has closed. Neptune's SaaS went dark on 5 March and hosted data was irreversibly deleted [[8]](https://docs.neptune.ai/transition_hub); the teams that had to move have moved. What is not a reason: the AI Act. High-risk obligations start on 2 December 2027 and 2 August 2028 [[16]](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ%3AL_202601744), and two of your target segments, defence and research, are outside its scope altogether [[17]](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/2/).
**Positioning:** Remove both claims that are no longer true — "the only one without Kubernetes" and "MLflow has no access control" — before somebody who knows the field reads the table. What remains true and is rare: one product from the training run to the running endpoint, with promotion by stage, immutable revisions and exact rollback, bought per seat with a card. The nearest equivalents each fail a small team somewhere else. ClearML's free server is several services you operate yourself: the install guide has you change a kernel parameter for Elasticsearch and warns that the server starts with unrestricted access [[1]](https://clear.ml/docs/latest/docs/deploying_clearml/clearml_server_linux_mac/), and the supported on-premises version is an Enterprise quote [[9]](https://clear.ml/pricing). Valohai needs credentials from its support team, a queue machine and an object store before the first worker starts [[3]](https://docs.valohai.com/installation-and-setup/on-premises/linux-workers), and publishes no price [[11]](https://valohai.com/pricing/). MLflow gives you tracking, a registry and a serving command, and leaves what connects them — which version is live on which machine, who promoted it, how to go back — to your own scripts [[4]](https://mlflow.org/docs/latest/ml/deployment/deploy-model-locally/). dstack runs endpoints on your own servers over SSH with no cluster, and has no registry and no tracking [[18]](https://dstack.ai/docs/concepts/services/). The sentence to test: "From a training run to a running endpoint on your own GPU box, with a rollback button and a price on the website." One decision the positioning hangs on is still open from August: is MolaBoard a hosted control plane with the customer's machines attached, or software the customer installs entirely on their side? The first is the shape ClearML and Valohai already have, and it cannot be sold to an air-gapped or classified site. The second can, and is more work.
**Whitespace / wedge:** The buyer nobody serves is a team of five to twenty inside a company of fifty or more employees, with its own GPU machines and a reason the data stays in the building. The self-serve plan of W&B excludes that company by headcount [[12]](https://coreweave.com/forge-pricing); the self-serve plans of ClearML and Comet stop at ten users and are SaaS [[9]](https://clear.ml/pricing) [[10]](https://www.comet.com/site/pricing/); everything on-premises starts with a sales call. Published prices run from $15–25 per user per month for hosted trackers to $250 per seat for the one new entrant that prices support in [[19]](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/PLM-pluto-oss-experiment-tracker-for-neptune-users) — a tenfold range with nothing in between, and nothing at all for self-hosted. The regulated version of the same buyer is medical imaging: 310 CE-marked radiology AI products in Europe, 107 of them still on old MDD certificates [[20]](https://healthairegister.com/radiology/) that have to be replaced by MDR certification by the end of 2027 or 2028 depending on class [[21]](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/607/oj). Under the MDR a change to an approved device needs the notified body's approval where it could affect safety or performance [[22]](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32017R0745), which makes a retrained model a change-control event with a paper trail. Your immutable revisions and the record of who changed a stage, and when, are that paper trail. You do not sell them as such yet.

### Who already runs tracking, registry and serving on your own machine — without Kubernetes
Three products do, and a fourth does half. The August survey said none did; that was wrong, and the correction changes what MolaBoard can claim. What separates them from each other is not what they can do but what it takes to start, and who you have to talk to first.
- ClearML Server (open source) deploys with docker-compose on a single Linux host: at least 8 GB of memory, ports 8080, 8081 and 8008, and a kernel parameter change for Elasticsearch. The guide warns that the server launches with unrestricted access until you secure it.
- ClearML Serving installs with pip and runs its containers with docker-compose; the prerequisites read 'Kubernetes / Single-instance Machine', and the Helm chart is optional. Licence Apache-2.0. Together with the server that is tracking, registry and serving on one box, with no cluster.
- Valohai on-premises: the worker agent ('Peon') runs as a systemd service on Ubuntu 24.04 with Docker and the NVIDIA Container Toolkit. No Kubernetes. It needs a queue address, a Redis password and an installer URL from Valohai's support team; the worker talks to a queue machine inside the customer's environment (Redis over TLS), and artifacts stay in the customer's object storage. Valohai never connects to the on-premises machine directly.
- That Valohai design is MolaBoard's 'outbound connections only, model files never pass through us' almost word for word. It is not a property you can claim as unique.
- MLflow (Apache-2.0, 28,762 GitHub stars): tracking, a registry with versions and aliases, and 'mlflow models serve' for a local scoring endpoint. Since 3.10 it has workspaces, and since 3.13 (29 May 2026) role-based access control with reusable roles, workspace-scoped grants and an admin interface, switched on by starting the server with authentication enabled.
- dstack (Munich, MPL-2.0, 2,310 stars): 'SSH fleets' turn existing servers into a pool with nothing but Linux, Docker and SSH — no cluster. Its services are inference endpoints with token authentication on by default, in-place rolling updates and optional autoscaling. It has no model registry and no experiment tracking. It was missing from the August survey.
- SkyPilot (Apache-2.0, 10,821 stars, 1.1 million downloads a month) covers the same orchestration ground across clouds and on-premises machines and raised a $20M seed in July 2026. Like dstack it schedules work and does not track or register models.
- Weights & Biases covers tracking and registry; since 30 September 2026 it is a product inside CoreWeave Forge, where training, inference and the registry are CoreWeave services. The pricing page offers private or dedicated deployment only on the Enterprise plan.
- What none of them offers a small team: a supported install on the team's own hardware that can be bought without a sales conversation. ClearML and Comet keep on-premises in the Enterprise tier, Valohai quotes individually, and MLflow and dstack are free and unsupported.
- Usage on 11 November 2026, PyPI downloads in the last month: MLflow 19.9M, wandb 14.5M, SkyPilot 1.13M, ClearML 0.77M, comet-ml 0.33M, trackio 0.31M, ZenML 0.20M, BentoML 0.13M, dstack 0.015M. The August survey quoted 40.3M for MLflow; that figure does not reproduce today and should not be reused.

### The release record as the product: which clauses ask for what MolaBoard already stores
For a regulated buyer the registry and the deployment log are not conveniences; they are evidence. This section maps what your product records onto the clauses that ask for it, and names what is missing.
- MDR Annex IX, 4.10: changes to an approved device require approval from the notified body where they could affect safety and performance. What MolaBoard holds: an immutable revision per deployment and a record of every stage change with the person and the time.
- MDR Art. 10(8): technical documentation is kept available for at least 10 years after the last device was placed on the market, 15 for implantables. What MolaBoard does not yet say: how long a release record is kept, and how the customer gets it out.
- MDR Annex I, 17.2: software is developed according to the state of the art, taking into account the development life cycle, risk management, verification and validation. What MolaBoard holds: the commit, the uncommitted changes as a patch, the hyperparameters and the console output of each run — enough to reproduce a model.
- MDCG 2025-6 (June 2025): a medical AI system can keep one set of technical documentation for the MDR and the AI Act, and changes the manufacturer planned and documented in advance are not a substantial modification. A registry that records the planned change and the executed one side by side is what that guidance asks for.
- AI Act Art. 12 requires automatic logging over the lifetime of a high-risk system, Annex IV a description of the changes made through its life cycle, and Art. 17 written procedures for managing modifications. These apply from 2 December 2027 (Annex III) and 2 August 2028 (products under Annex I, medical devices included).
- Missing for an auditor: an export of the release record as a file that can be filed and signed; a stated retention period; and dataset versions, which app.md lists as a placeholder. Without the data a model was trained on, the record explains the release and not the model.
- The MDR is being revised (procedure 2025/0404(COD)): about 800 amendments were tabled in Parliament in July 2026, the committee vote is scheduled for 3 December, and the Council aims at a general approach by the end of the year. The proposal would move medical devices to Section B of Annex I of the AI Act, so AI in devices would be governed mainly by the MDR. Until it is adopted, the current MDR applies.

### Regulation: the MDR is in force, the AI Act is not yet, and the Cyber Resilience Act now applies to you
One regulation gives a customer a reason to buy today, one is two years away, and one — new since the August survey — puts obligations on MolaBoard itself.
- In force for the customer: the MDR, applicable since 2021, with the change-approval and record-keeping duties set out in the section above.
- Not yet in force: the AI Act's high-risk obligations, postponed by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 to 2 December 2027 for Annex III systems and 2 August 2028 for products under Annex I. A pitch that says 'AI Act compliance' is selling a 2028 problem.
- Out of scope of the AI Act altogether: systems used exclusively for military, defence or national security purposes (Art. 2(3)) and systems developed solely for scientific research and development (Art. 2(6)). Two of your five target segments have no AI Act reason to buy.
- What moves those two segments instead: GDPR Chapter V, which makes any processing outside the EU a documented transfer assessment, and Council Decision 2013/488/EU, which requires accreditation of systems handling EU classified information.
- New and on your side of the table: the Cyber Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847). Since 11 September 2026 manufacturers of products with digital elements must report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents to the national CSIRT and ENISA within 24 hours, including for products already on the market. The remaining obligations — secure development, vulnerability handling, documentation, CE marking — apply from 11 December 2027.
- Software you sell to be installed on a customer's machine is such a product. The Commission published guidance on the Act's application on 27 July 2026; read the part on remote data processing before you decide how much of MolaBoard runs on your side.
- For a regulated or public buyer the same Act is a question in the tender: who is the manufacturer, how do we learn about a vulnerability, and for how long are fixes provided. Have the three answers written down.

### Market size: count downloads, revenue and teams — not analyst reports
Top-down figures for 'the MLOps market' disagree by a factor of two in the base year and are of no use for a plan. Three things can be counted: how much the tools are used, what the independent vendors earn, and what a team would pay.
- The analyst spread, as checked in August: the same market in the same base year sized between $1.97B and $4.0B. A report published on 6 November 2026 adds a forecast of $52.29B by 2036 at 20.7% a year. Use none of them as an input.
- Usage: MLflow 19.9 million downloads a month, wandb 14.5 million, ClearML 0.77 million (PyPI, 11 November 2026). Two tools hold nearly all the usage, and one of them is free.
- Revenue of the largest independent platform vendors, per Sacra: Dataiku $342.5M in annual recurring revenue (September 2025) from about 500 customers, DataRobot $225M (2023). Together well under $1B — and both sell to large enterprises, not to teams of ten.
- Bottom-up for your niche: the August survey estimated 3,000–10,000 organisations in the EU that own GPUs and have a reason to keep data in-house. No source measures GPU ownership, so the range is an estimate, not a count.
- At published per-seat prices, a ten-seat team is worth $1,800–30,000 a year. Multiplied through, the whole EU niche is somewhere between $5M and $300M a year, most plausibly in the tens of millions. That is a market for a profitable small company or a first product, and not yet a venture-scale story on its own.
- The one countable regulated sub-segment: 310 CE-marked radiology AI products from roughly 120 companies (the company count is from August). Ten seats at $100 a month from every one of them is about $1.4M a year in total — a reference market that proves the audit-trail argument, not the market itself.

### Why workloads move to owned compute — and which arguments do not hold
The move is real among large enterprises and driven by control and cost predictability. Whether it reaches the small teams MolaBoard is built for is not shown by any survey.
- Broadcom Private Cloud Outlook 2026 (Radius Tech for Broadcom; 1,800 senior IT decision-makers at organisations with 1,000 or more employees in eight countries; fielded February–March 2026): 56% run or plan to run production AI inference on private cloud; public cloud's share fell 15 points to 41%.
- Same survey: 83% are considering moving workloads from public to private cloud and 50% have moved at least some; cost has overtaken security as the first concern about public cloud, rising from 26% to 31%.
- Two caveats. Broadcom sells the private cloud software, and the 83% counts companies, not workloads: one small application moved back is enough to be counted.
- The tracker market itself is being tied to compute. W&B is now part of a GPU cloud's platform, Anyscale is being bought by another, and Replicate belongs to Cloudflare. For a team that owns its machines, a tool sold by a compute vendor has an obvious conflict of interest — this is the form of the sovereignty argument that applies to you.
- An argument that does not hold: price. As recorded in August, the large clouds cut GPU rental prices sharply in 2025, and owning a card pays back only at high, sustained utilisation. Sell control and continuity, not savings.
- An argument that does not hold either: 'everybody is leaving W&B'. 14.5 million downloads a month five weeks after the Forge launch says they are not.

### Funding and M&A: money goes to compute and inference; lifecycle tools get absorbed
Nothing in the last three months changes the August picture; two rounds confirm it. Investors fund whoever sells compute by the second, and the tools around the model end up inside those companies.
- Baseten: $1.5B Series F announced on 22 June 2026, led by Altimeter, Conviction and Spark, in two tranches at valuations of $13B and $11B — five months after a $300M Series E at $5B.
- Modal: $355M Series C at a $4.65B valuation (21 May 2026).
- Acquisitions of lifecycle tools, with the buyer's business: Qwak by JFrog (2024, $230M — the only price confirmed by a buyer), Weights & Biases by CoreWeave (completed May 2025), Replicate by Cloudflare (announced November 2025), Neptune by OpenAI (December 2025), Seldon by TrueFoundry (June 2026), Anyscale by Nscale (announced July 2026, not yet closed; the press reports $1.65B, Nscale disclosed no terms).
- What gets funded on the orchestration side: SkyPilot raised a $20M seed in July 2026. The open-source project you most resemble in deployment model, dstack, has raised very little that is public.
- Pluto, the tracker launched for Neptune's users, is a product of Trainy, a Y Combinator company that manages GPU infrastructure — a side product of an existing company, not a newly funded start-up.
- Reading for a raise: a seed investor will ask why this is a company and not a feature of one of the above. The answer is a paying segment nobody else can serve with their current price list — which is why the first design partner matters more than the deck.
