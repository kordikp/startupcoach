# ML model lifecycle management on customer-owned hardware

**Founder:** @lauritom  ·  **Project:** startup — https://gitlab.fit.cvut.cz/lauritom/startup

> How can a platform unify ML training tracking, model versioning, and deployment to customer-owned compute infrastructure, addressing the gap where existing experiment trackers stop at the model registry?

**Tags:** `experiment tracking`, `model registry`, `on-premise deployment`, `bring-your-own compute`, `serving contracts`

## 📊 Market intelligence

MolaBoard's stated differentiator is already table stakes. Weights & Biases, Comet, ClearML, Valohai, ZenML, Hopsworks, TrueFoundry, Domino, DataRobot, UbiOps, Anyscale and Iguazio all ship customer-hosted deployment, seven of them explicitly air-gapped [[1]](https://docs.wandb.ai/guides/hosting/self-managed/) [[2]](https://valohai.com/pricing/) [[3]](https://docs.hopsworks.ai/latest/) [[4]](https://www.truefoundry.com/docs/deploy-truefoundry-in-an-air-gapped-environment); even Google runs a genuinely air-gapped Vertex AI inside customer data centres [[5]](https://docs.cloud.google.com/distributed-cloud/hosted/docs/latest/gdcag/application/ao-user/vertex-ai-overview). Only AWS SageMaker and Modal have no customer-hardware path at all [[6]](https://modal.com/pricing). But the research also found the real gap, and MolaBoard is already sitting in it: nobody covers tracking AND registry AND serving in one self-hostable install that does NOT require Kubernetes, a Helm chart and an enterprise sales contract. W&B, Comet and Determined have no serving; BentoML, Seldon and Modal have no tracking; every self-hosted serving layer in the field is Kubernetes-bound — KServe needs a cluster, Kubeflow's Model Registry is still alpha [[7]](https://github.com/kubeflow/model-registry), BentoML's Yatai operator was archived in June 2026 [[8]](https://github.com/bentoml/Yatai), and Seldon Core 2 left open source for a Business Source License [[9]](https://github.com/SeldonIO/seldon-core). The commercial reality is harsher than the analyst reports suggest: six firms size this market between $1.97B and $4.0B (a 2x spread) [[10]](https://www.imarcgroup.com/mlops-market) [[11]](https://www.skyquestt.com/report/mlops-market), yet the largest independent vendors together clear under $1B in real revenue — Dataiku $342.5M ARR, DataRobot $225M [[12]](https://sacra.com/c/dataiku/) — and the default competitor is free, with MLflow drawing ~40.3M PyPI downloads a month against ClearML's 0.58M [[13]](https://pypistats.org/api/packages/mlflow/recent). The winning story is therefore not sovereignty but survivability plus simplicity: every neutral MLOps tool in this window was swallowed by a company selling something else, and Neptune's users learned what that costs when OpenAI shut the service down and irreversibly deleted hosted data in March 2026 [[14]](https://docs.neptune.ai/transition_hub).

**Why now:** Three forces converge right now. (1) Production inference is genuinely moving to customer-controlled compute: Broadcom's Private Cloud Outlook 2026 (n=1,800 senior IT decision-makers, fielded Feb-Mar 2026) found public cloud's share of production AI inferencing fell 15 points year over year, from 56% to 41%, while 56% of enterprises now run or plan production inference on private cloud [[15]](https://news.broadcom.com/releases/broadcom-private-cloud-outlook-2026), with data sovereignty and residency (54%) now the leading geopolitical factor in infrastructure decisions; Gartner puts sovereign cloud IaaS spending at $80B in 2026, up 35.6% [[16]](https://www.ciodive.com/news/global-sovereign-cloud-spend-increase-2026/811896/), and the EU is subsidising non-hyperscaler compute through 19 AI Factories plus 13 antennas, including a Czech one [[17]](https://www.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu/ai-factories_en). (2) The neutral-tooling layer is being consolidated away: W&B to CoreWeave (May 2025), Run:ai to Nvidia, Qwak to JFrog ($230M, the only confirmed price) [[18]](https://www.itpro.com/business/acquisition/jfrog-to-acquire-ai-platform-qwak-for-dollar230-million), Seldon to TrueFoundry (June 2026) [[19]](https://siliconangle.com/2026/06/25/truefoundry-acquires-mlops-pioneer-seldon-ai-accelerate-enterprise-agentic-ai/), Anyscale to Nscale (July 2026) [[20]](https://www.nscale.com/press-releases/nscale-acquires-anyscale) and Neptune to OpenAI — creating a live population of displaced, actively-migrating users right now [[21]](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4101200/openai-to-acquire-ai-training-tracker-neptune.html). (3) The self-hostable alternatives are decaying at the same moment: Seldon Core 2 relicensed away from open source, BentoML's Yatai archived, Kubeflow's Model Registry still alpha, and Determined AI has shipped no release since v0.38.1 in March 2025 [[22]](https://github.com/determined-ai/determined/releases). Be honest about what is NOT driving this: GPU cost arbitrage has weakened, since AWS cut H100 rental ~44% in June 2025 and neoclouds run 3-5x cheaper than the big three [[23]](https://www.cloudzero.com/blog/h100-gpu-cost/), and the 'open weights force self-hosting' thesis is contradicted by open-source models falling to 11% of enterprise LLM usage from 19% [[24]](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise/). The driver is control and continuity, not price.
**Positioning:** Stop selling 'runs on your own hardware' — every serious competitor already does, so the claim reads as parity, not advantage, to any buyer who has shopped around. Sell instead: 'tracking, registry and deployment in ONE install, on a single GPU box or workstation, with no Kubernetes cluster, no Helm chart and no enterprise sales call.' Each clause of that sentence is a documented gap. Coverage: W&B, Comet and Determined have no serving at all [[25]](https://docs.wandb.ai/guides/inference/), while BentoML, Seldon, Modal and Ray have no experiment tracking, and Google's air-gapped Vertex ships inference with neither registry nor tracking. Kubernetes-free: KServe requires a cluster, BentoCloud's BYOC supports only AWS/GCP/Azure/OCI VPCs rather than bare metal [[26]](https://docs.bentoml.com/en/latest/scale-with-bentocloud/administering/bring-your-own-cloud.html), and Anyscale's on-prem path needs a Kubernetes operator [[27]](https://docs.anyscale.com/clouds/kubernetes) — which leaves the workstation and single bare-metal server structurally unserved, exactly the deployment targets already named in MolaBoard's own docs (workstation, on-prem GPU server, customer VM, Mac mini). Self-serve pricing: not one vendor in this segment publishes an on-prem or air-gapped licence price — ClearML, Valohai, Hopsworks, W&B Advanced Enterprise, TrueFoundry, Domino, Dataiku and DataRobot are all contact-sales [[28]](https://clear.ml/pricing) [[29]](https://www.hopsworks.ai/pricing), so a published price for self-hosted deployment would itself be a differentiator for the 5-20 person teams that fall between the ~10-seat self-serve caps and the enterprise quote. Second pillar, equally defensible: neutrality. Every neutral tool in this window was acquired by a GPU cloud, a chip vendor, a frontier lab or a devops incumbent; an independent tool that cannot delete a customer's data because it never held it is the structural answer to what happened to Neptune's users.
**Whitespace / wedge:** The ownable wedge is the small GPU-owning team that MLflow currently serves badly and the enterprise vendors do not serve at all: 5-20 people, one or a few GPU machines, no platform team, no Kubernetes, and a hard reason their data cannot leave the building. Today that team's realistic options are (a) stitch MLflow together with custom serving glue — free, and the genuine incumbent at ~40.3M PyPI downloads a month [[13]](https://pypistats.org/api/packages/mlflow/recent) — or (b) enter an enterprise sales process for a Kubernetes-native platform with an unpublished price. Nothing occupies the middle. The concrete product shape that owns it: a single-binary or single-compose install that brings up tracking, registry and serving on one box; a machine joins with one command (already MolaBoard's design); promotion by stage rather than by pinned version so deployments follow the registry; and immutable, auditable release records. That last property is where the second market is: MDR has required notified-body approval for changes to an approved device and 10-15 year documentation retention since 2021 [[30]](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32017R0745), which makes every retrain of a hospital-deployed model a change-control event with an audit trail attached — an in-force buying reason, unlike the AI Act, whose high-risk obligations were postponed to December 2027 and August 2028 by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 [[31]](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ%3AL_202601744) and which excludes defence and pure research entirely [[32]](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/2/). Medical imaging is therefore the sharpest regulated beachhead — 119 companies hold CE-marked radiology AI products [[33]](https://radiology.healthairegister.com/) — while for research labs and defence the argument is GDPR Chapter V transfer assessments [[34]](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-44-gdpr/) and classified-system accreditation [[35]](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32013D0488), not AI Act compliance.

### ✅ Strategic recommendations
- Rewrite the one-liner this week. Drop 'deploy to hardware you own' as the headline — it is parity, not differentiation, and every informed buyer will know it. Lead with 'tracking, registry and deploy in one install on a single GPU box — no Kubernetes, no sales call.' Then test that sentence on the next five conversations and count how many ask a follow-up question rather than nodding politely.
- Go after Neptune's displaced users NOW — this is the sharpest, most time-boxed opportunity in this report. OpenAI shut the Neptune SaaS down in March 2026 and irreversibly deleted hosted data [[14]](https://docs.neptune.ai/transition_hub); those teams are mid-migration, have an unwilling reason to move, and were explicitly pointed at MLflow, W&B and Comet. They are the design partners you asked for, and the window closes as they finish migrating.
- Validate the ACV assumption before believing any TAM. The bottom-up sizing here assumes $50k-200k per customer, but published self-serve pricing for a 10-person team is $720-6,000/year and NOT ONE vendor publishes an on-prem price [[28]](https://clear.ml/pricing) [[2]](https://valohai.com/pricing/). Ask each design partner the two questions that settle it: what do you pay today for this, and what would you pay for a supported self-hosted install? Until then treat the TAM as a hypothesis, not a number for a deck.
- Publish a price for self-hosted deployment. It is the one competitive move nobody in this segment has made — ClearML, Valohai, Hopsworks, W&B, TrueFoundry, Domino, Dataiku and DataRobot are all contact-sales for on-prem [[29]](https://www.hopsworks.ai/pricing) — and it directly serves the 5-20 person teams stranded between the ~10-seat self-serve caps and the enterprise quote.
- Make medical imaging the regulated beachhead, not 'AI Act compliance'. MDR has been in force since 2021, requires notified-body approval for changes to an approved device and 10-15 year documentation retention [[30]](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32017R0745), so auditable model releases are already a legal requirement rather than a future one. Carebot (chest X-ray AI in 500+ hospitals) is in your contact list and is exactly this buyer.
- Drop the AI Act from the pitch to research labs and defence. Article 2 excludes both — military/defence (2(3)) and scientific R&D (2(6)/(8)) [[32]](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/2/) — and Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 pushed high-risk obligations to Dec 2027/Aug 2028. For research the argument is GDPR Chapter V transfer assessments; for defence it is that classified systems must be accredited, which vendor-hosted SaaS fails by construction.
- Benchmark against Valohai and ClearML honestly, in writing, before your first investor conversation. Valohai already covers tracking, versioned registry with approvals, and K8s endpoint promotion, on-prem and air-gapped, across all subscriptions [[2]](https://valohai.com/pricing/). If you cannot state in one sentence what a team gets from you that they do not get from Valohai or free ClearML, that is the gap to close before raising.
- Treat MLflow, not the commercial vendors, as the competitor to beat. At ~40.3M PyPI downloads a month versus ClearML's 0.58M [[13]](https://pypistats.org/api/packages/mlflow/recent), free-and-good-enough is what you actually displace. Have a crisp answer to 'why not just MLflow plus a serving script?' — ideally an import path from MLflow rather than an argument against it.

### ⚠️ Key risks
- The stated differentiator is not one. On-prem/self-hosted is documented by W&B, Comet, ClearML, Valohai, ZenML, Hopsworks, TrueFoundry, Domino, DataRobot, UbiOps, Anyscale and Iguazio, with air-gapped support at seven of them. Any pitch resting on 'we run on your hardware' will be dismissed by an informed buyer in the first meeting.
- The default competitor is free and dominant. MLflow is Apache-2.0, covers tracking, registry AND local serving via `mlflow models serve`, and outdraws every commercial tool combined (~40.3M monthly PyPI downloads vs ClearML's 0.58M). Displacing 'free and good enough' with a paid product and no distribution is the central commercial risk.
- Willingness to pay is unproven in exactly this segment. There is no public on-prem price anywhere in the market to benchmark against, and published self-serve tiers cap around 10 users at $15-19/user/month. The $50k-200k ACV underlying the bottom-up TAM is an assumption with zero external validation.
- Zero external users at a moment of active consolidation. Neptune is gone, Seldon is inside TrueFoundry, Anyscale inside Nscale, W&B inside CoreWeave. Buyers in this category are visibly nervous about vendor continuity, and a pre-alpha with no reference customers is the least reassuring option on the shortlist.
- The market may be much smaller than analyst reports imply. The independent vendor revenue base is under $1B combined (Dataiku $342.5M, DataRobot $225M) against claimed market sizes of $2-4B, and the EU population that owns GPUs AND faces a residency constraint counts in the low thousands (roughly 3,000-10,000 organisations), not hundreds of thousands.
- A well-funded competitor can close the Kubernetes-free gap faster than a solo pre-alpha can build distribution. Modal raised $355M at $4.65B and Baseten is reportedly raising at $11-13B; either could ship a single-node self-hosted install as a feature, and TrueFoundry already has the air-gapped install plus published pricing.
- Two of the three named target segments have no regulatory pull. Defence and scientific R&D are explicitly outside the AI Act, so the compliance narrative does not travel there; and even for those in scope, high-risk obligations were postponed to Dec 2027/Aug 2028, removing the 2026 urgency the pitch might otherwise assume.
- Founder-market timing risk on the cost argument. If the pitch leans on GPU economics, it is now weaker than it was: AWS cut H100 rental ~44% in June 2025 and neoclouds are 3-5x cheaper than the big three, while owning an H100 needs 18+ months of near-full utilisation to break even.

### On-prem is table stakes; the wedge is Kubernetes-free single-node deployment for small GPU-owning teams
On-premises / self-hosted deployment is NOT a differentiator in this market — it is table stakes and has been for years. W&B, Comet, ClearML, Valohai, ZenML, Hopsworks, TrueFoundry, Domino, DataRobot, UbiOps, Anyscale and Iguazio all document customer-hosted deployment, and seven of them explicitly document air-gapped installs; even Google runs an air-gapped Vertex AI on customer hardware. Only AWS SageMaker and Modal have no customer-hardware path at all. Two things are genuinely scarce: (1) covering tracking AND registry AND serving in one self-hostable install — W&B, Comet, Neptune and Determined have no serving, while BentoML, Seldon, Modal and Ray have no experiment tracking; and (2) an on-prem path that does not require a Kubernetes cluster, a Helm chart and an enterprise sales contract. The credible wedge is therefore not 'we run on your hardware' but 'tracking to registry to deploy in one install, self-serve priced, on a single bare-metal GPU box or workstation, with no Kubernetes and no enterprise contract'. That wedge is widening, not closing.
- Weights & Biases Self-Managed explicitly supports on-premises and air-gapped installs (OpenShift Kubernetes plus a dedicated air-gapped operator path), yet W&B — owned by CoreWeave since 5 May 2025 — has no way to serve a customer-trained model: W&B Inference is a pay-per-token endpoint for third-party open-source foundation models on CoreWeave's own GPUs.
- MLflow is Apache-2.0 and is the only fully free option covering all three layers — experiment tracking, a Model Registry with versions and aliases, and serving via a single `mlflow models serve` command starting a FastAPI scoring endpoint, with Docker, Kubernetes and SageMaker as further targets. This is the true default competitor.
- ClearML is the closest free analogue — tracking, model repository, and ClearML Serving (Apache-2.0, Triton-backed, auto-deploys on model publish) — but its server is SSPL v1 rather than Apache, Pro is $15/user/month, and air-gapped deployment is gated to Scale/Enterprise custom-quote tiers.
- Valohai has the least-gated on-prem story in the tracking tier, deploying 'as a fully managed cloud service, on-premises, in a private cloud, or in air-gapped environments' across ALL subscriptions, covering tracking, a versioned model registry and Kubernetes-backed serving endpoints — but publishes no prices, selling only per-user quotes.
- TrueFoundry acquired Seldon AI on 24 June 2026 and is the most direct architectural competitor: a documented air-gapped install (mirror images to a private OCI registry, then Helm install) covering tracking, versioned model registry and deployment, priced publicly at $499/month Pro and $2,999/month Pro Plus.
- Hopsworks documents installation 'On-Prem (Ubuntu/Redhat compatible), even in air-gapped data centers' covering feature store, model registry and KServe-based serving — though it is AGPL-v3 and its free tier excludes serving entirely.
- ZenML is Apache-2.0 and self-hostable via Docker or Helm for free, with ZenML Pro sold as 'an air-gapped solution on your infrastructure' (SaaS Scale $999/month) — but ZenML has no native serving and delegates deployment to third-party Model Deployer integrations.
- Comet includes a model registry at every tier at $19/user/month, but on-premises deployment is Enterprise-only and Comet offers NO model serving at all; only Opik, its LLM-observability product, is open source.
- EVERY self-hosted serving layer in this field is Kubernetes-bound: KServe requires a cluster and is an ecosystem project rather than a core Kubeflow subproject (Kubeflow's own Model Registry still carries 'alpha status with limited support'); BentoCloud's BYOC supports only AWS/GCP/Azure/OCI VPCs rather than bare metal and its Yatai self-hosting operator was archived in June 2026; Anyscale's on-prem option requires a Kubernetes cluster running the Anyscale operator. Single-node bare-metal and workstation deployment is structurally under-served.
- The wedge is widening rather than closing: Seldon Core 2 is no longer open source (LICENSE is Business Source License 1.1 with a four-year change date to Apache-2.0) and is Kubernetes-native serving only, with no tracking and no registry.
- Determined AI, though Apache-2.0 and installable on bare metal, Kubernetes or Slurm/PBS, has no serving component and has not shipped a release since v0.38.1 on 19 March 2025; HPE's commercial MLDE build likewise offers training and tracking but no inference.
- Neptune.ai was acquired by OpenAI and shut down: the hosted app and API were turned off at 10am PST on 5 March 2026 and its Helm and container image repositories were deleted on 8 March 2026, removing a named competitor from the board entirely — and stranding its customers.
- Modal is the clearest counterexample to on-prem being universal: it publishes exact serverless GPU rates (H100 $0.001097/GPU-second, A100-80GB $0.000694, Team plan $250/month) and documents no self-hosted or bring-your-own-hardware path whatsoever.
- The enterprise tier treats on-prem as a default rather than a feature: Domino deploys 'in your VPC — on AWS, Azure, GCP, or on-premises' with 'secure, air-gapped environments'; DataRobot lists 'On-Premise, Virtual Private Cloud, Software as a Service' and ships a Portable Prediction Server as a self-contained Docker image; Iguazio (acquired by McKinsey/QuantumBlack) advertises 'Multi-cloud, on-premises and hybrid environments all supported'.

### What is pushing ML workloads onto customer-owned compute (2025-2026)
Two large 2026 surveys point the same way: Broadcom's Private Cloud Outlook 2026 (n=1,800 senior IT decision-makers) found public cloud's share as the venue for production AI inference fell 15 points year over year, from 56% to 41%, while 56% of enterprises now run or plan production inference on private cloud; Cloudian's February 2026 survey (n=203, vendor-commissioned) found 93% have repatriated AI workloads, are doing so, or are evaluating it. The stated drivers are consistent: data sovereignty/residency, unpredictable cloud cost, and latency that cloud round-trips cannot meet. Sovereignty is now a budget line — Gartner puts sovereign cloud IaaS spending at $80B in 2026, up 35.6%. Crucially, the pure GPU-economics case for owning hardware has WEAKENED since mid-2025 because hyperscalers cut H100 rental prices sharply (AWS ~44% in June 2025), so the durable driver is control and locality, not cost arbitrage. The weakest leg of the thesis is 'open weights make self-hosting inevitable': Menlo Ventures measured open-source models falling to 11% of enterprise LLM usage from 19%.
- Broadcom's Private Cloud Outlook 2026 (1,800 senior IT decision-makers at 1,000+ employee organisations across eight countries, fielded February-March 2026) reports public cloud use for production AI inferencing dropped 15 percentage points year over year, from 56% to 41%, while 56% of enterprises now run or plan to run production AI inferencing on private cloud — the most direct available datapoint on ML workloads moving to customer-controlled compute.
- The same Broadcom survey found 83% of enterprises are considering moving workloads from public to private cloud and 50% have already repatriated at least some, with top drivers security and compliance (51%), cost predictability (39%) and performance (39%).
- Broadcom also found data sovereignty and residency requirements (54%) have overtaken jurisdiction-specific compliance (51%) as the leading geopolitical factor shaping infrastructure decisions, with four out of five IT leaders saying geopolitics now affects their IT strategy.
- Cloudian's Enterprise AI Infrastructure Survey (203 IT decision-makers, February 2026) found 93% of enterprises have repatriated some AI workloads, are in the process, or are actively evaluating it, with 79% having already moved workloads — note Cloudian sells on-premises storage, so this is vendor-commissioned.
- In the same Cloudian survey, 91% said they would choose on-premises, private cloud or hybrid for sensitive-data AI deployments, 58% reported data-residency concerns have already delayed or scaled back AI initiatives, and 75% identified AI workloads that require or would benefit from on-premises infrastructure for acceptable latency.
- Gartner's February 2026 forecast puts worldwide sovereign cloud IaaS spending at $80 billion in 2026, a 35.6% increase, with EMEA and Asia-Pacific recording the highest growth rates.

### MDR is the real buying reason; the AI Act just slipped to 2027/2028 and excludes defence and research
The only regulation that forces an auditable model-release trail TODAY is medical device law: MDR/IVDR have applied since 2021, require software built to a documented development lifecycle, require notified-body approval for changes to an approved device, and require technical documentation retained 10-15 years — so every retrain of a hospital-deployed model is already a change-control event with an audit trail attached. The EU AI Act would have been the second hard driver, but Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (Digital Omnibus on AI, in force 27 July 2026) pushed high-risk obligations to 2 December 2027 (Annex III) and 2 August 2028 (Annex I, incl. medical devices), turning it from a 2026 procurement trigger into a roadmap argument. Two of the stated target segments are explicitly carved OUT of the AI Act — defence (Art. 2(3)) and scientific R&D (Art. 2(6)/(8)) — so AI Act compliance is not a sellable reason to research labs or defence primes. What drives those two on-prem is different: EU classified-information rules require accredited systems, and GDPR Chapter V makes any cross-border SaaS processing a documented transfer assessment. ISO/IEC 42001, ISO/IEC 23894 and NIST AI RMF are voluntary — RFP checkboxes and vocabulary, not purchase mandates.
- TIMELINE CORRECTION: Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 of 8 July 2026 ('Digital Omnibus on AI'), in force from 27 July 2026, amends the AI Act to postpone high-risk obligations to 2 December 2027 for Annex III systems and 2 August 2028 for Annex I systems (which include AI in medical devices) — so no AI Act high-risk record-keeping duty bites in 2026. Any pitch built on 'AI Act compliance in 2026' is factually wrong.
- SEGMENT EXCLUSION: The AI Act does not apply to systems used 'exclusively for military, defence or national security purposes, regardless of the type of entity' (Art. 2(3)), nor to systems 'specifically developed and put into service for the sole purpose of scientific research and development' (Art. 2(6)), nor to R&D activity prior to market placement (Art. 2(8)) — meaning the defence and research-lab segments have NO AI Act-derived buying reason.
- MDR (Regulation (EU) 2017/745), applicable since 2021, Annex I section 17.2 requires that software be 'developed and manufactured in accordance with the state of the art taking into account the principles of development life cycle, risk management, including information security, verification and validation', and 17.4 requires manufacturers to specify minimum hardware, IT network and IT security requirements.
- MDR Annex IX section 4.10: 'Changes to the approved device shall require approval from the notified body which issued the EU technical documentation assessment certificate where such changes could affect the safety and performance of the device'; MDR Article 10(8) requires technical documentation kept available for at least 10 years after the last device was placed on the market (15 for implantables) — the concrete, IN-FORCE reason a hospital-facing model needs a durable, auditable release record.
- MDR Articles 83-86 require a documented post-market surveillance system 'suited to actively and systematically gathering, recording and analysing relevant data on the quality, performance and safety of a device throughout its entire lifetime', with periodic safety update reports for class IIa/IIb/III devices.
- Joint guidance MDCG 2025-6 / AIB 2025-1 (19 June 2025) states a medical device AI system is high-risk under AI Act Art. 6(1) only if it is a device or safety component AND subject to notified-body conformity assessment, that Art. 11(2) permits 'a single set of technical documentation', and that changes pre-determined by the manufacturer and documented per AI Act Annex IV point 2(f) do not constitute a substantial modification.
- AI Act Article 12 requires high-risk systems to 'technically allow for the automatic recording of events (logs) over the lifetime of the system' — an engineered capability, not manual documentation — while Art. 19 obliges providers and Art. 26(6) obliges deployers to retain those logs for at least six months.
- AI Act Annex IV technical documentation must include version history, a 'description of relevant changes made by the provider to the system through its lifecycle', datasheets on training methodologies and datasets, pre-determined changes with technical solutions ensuring continued compliance, and 'test logs and all test reports dated and signed by the responsible persons'; Art. 11 requires it drawn up before market placement and 'kept up-to date'.
- AI Act Article 17 requires the provider's quality management system to include 'procedures for the management of modifications' (point a) and 'systems and procedures for record-keeping of all relevant documentation and information' (point k) — the clause that maps most directly onto a model registry plus deployment log.
- AI Act Article 73 sets serious-incident reporting deadlines of 15 days normally, not later than 2 days for widespread infringement or critical-infrastructure cases, and 10 days where a death is involved, requiring investigation while avoiding alterations affecting later evaluation — which in practice demands frozen, reproducible model versions tied to the deployment that produced the incident.
- GDPR Article 44 permits third-country transfers only under Chapter V, and EDPB Recommendations 01/2020 (final, 18 June 2021) require exporters to run a documented step-by-step assessment of third-country law and supplementary measures — running training and inference on customer-owned hardware inside the EU removes that assessment entirely, which is the sovereignty argument that actually closes deals in hospitals and public research.
- For defence and classified work, Council Decision 2013/488/EU requires all communication and information systems handling EU classified information to undergo formal accreditation fixing the maximum classification level, with no provision for routine processing of classified information on non-accredited systems — a hard architectural bar any vendor-hosted SaaS fails by construction.
- DORA (Regulation (EU) 2022/2554) requires financial entities to maintain a register of all ICT service contracts, specify processing locations and access/audit rights, and under Art. 28(8) have dedicated exit strategies — a strong on-prem driver for a segment (finance) not currently targeted.
- ISO/IEC 42001 and ISO/IEC 23894 are voluntary certifiable/guidance standards covering the AI lifecycle; NIST AI RMF 1.0 is explicitly 'intended for voluntary use' and its playbook defines GOVERN 1.6 as maintaining an AI system inventory — 'an organized database of artifacts relating to an AI system or model' — with GOVERN 1.7 requiring documented decommissioning policies. These function as RFP checkboxes and shared vocabulary, not purchase mandates.

### Every MLOps tool in this window was absorbed by a company selling something else
The MLOps tooling layer is being absorbed rather than funded: in the last ~24 months every significant experiment-tracking, model-serving or orchestration vendor was bought by a company that sells something else — GPU clouds (CoreWeave/Weights & Biases, Nscale/Anyscale), chip vendors (Nvidia/Run:ai), frontier labs (OpenAI/Neptune.ai) and devops incumbents (JFrog/Qwak, TrueFoundry/Seldon). Prices are mostly undisclosed or only 'reported': the only acquirer-confirmed figure in the window is JFrog's $230M for Qwak. Meanwhile the money that IS being raised goes to compute and serving, not lifecycle tooling: Modal raised $355M at $4.65B post-money in May 2026, and Baseten went from a $5B Series E in January 2026 to a reported $1.5B round at $11-13B in June 2026. Pure-play MLOps tooling companies remain small — ZenML's total seed is $6.4M, ClearML roughly $11-16M disclosed. The most consequential precedent is Neptune.ai: OpenAI acquired it in December 2025 and the SaaS was shut down on 4-5 March 2026 with hosted data deleted, stranding customers and demonstrating the acquisition risk baked into vendor-hosted ML tooling.
- CoreWeave completed its acquisition of Weights & Biases on 5 May 2025 (announced 4 March 2025); CoreWeave's own press release discloses no price, and the widely quoted $1.7 billion figure comes from reporting by The Information rather than either company.
- Weights & Biases was last valued at $1.25 billion in 2023 and served over 1,400 organisations including AstraZeneca and Nvidia — implying a reported exit at roughly 1.4x its last private valuation, though no revenue multiple is public.
- TrueFoundry announced the acquisition of Seldon Technologies on 25 June 2026 for an undisclosed price; Seldon (founded 2014) had raised approximately $33 million from AlbionVC, Bright Pixel and Cambridge Innovation Capital, with PayPal, Johnson & Johnson, Audi and Experian as customers.
- OpenAI announced a definitive agreement to acquire Neptune.ai on 4 December 2025; terms were not disclosed, and the neptune.ai domain now 308-redirects to OpenAI's announcement page — reports of a sub-$400M value in OpenAI stock circulate in secondary outlets but are not confirmed.
- THE CAUTIONARY TALE: Neptune's product was withdrawn rather than continued — the SaaS platform shut down in March 2026 with all hosted data 'securely and irreversibly deleted', self-hosted customers handled individually by account managers, and users pointed at MLflow, W&B, Comet or hyperscaler platforms as migration targets.
- Analysts read the Neptune deal as vendor capture of neutral tooling: Faisal Kawoosa of Techarc said 'testing, experiment tracking tooling, etc., should not be linked or aligned to any vendor of tech including AI', while Moor Insights' Anshel Sag framed it as OpenAI ensuring 'its favorite tools are always available for it to use'.
- Nvidia closed its acquisition of Run:ai on 2 January 2025 at a reported $700 million (not confirmed by Nvidia) and open-sourced the software; the European Commission cleared the deal in December 2024 on the grounds that Run:ai's revenue was negligible.
- JFrog announced the acquisition of Qwak on 25-26 June 2024 for $230 million — the only acquirer-confirmed price among MLOps deals in this window — folding Qwak's training, serving, feature store and deployment stack into JFrog ML with Artifactory as the model registry.
- Nscale announced an agreement to acquire Anyscale (the company behind Ray) on 30 July 2026, terms undisclosed, closing expected H2 2026; Anyscale's last disclosed round was a $100M Series C at a $1B valuation, and Ray was donated to the PyTorch Foundation in 2025.
- Nscale's press release explicitly preserves infrastructure neutrality — 'customers will remain free to choose the infrastructure on which they run their AI workloads' — the same positioning CoreWeave used for W&B, indicating buyers know hardware lock-in is the main objection to compute-vendor-owned tooling.
- Modal raised a $355 million Series C at a $4.65 billion post-money valuation (21 May 2026), led by General Catalyst and Redpoint, on more than $300 million annualised revenue and fivefold growth since Series B — roughly a 15x revenue multiple.
- Baseten raised a $300 million Series E at a $5 billion valuation around January 2026 and, per TechCrunch (18 June 2026), was close to finalising a reported $1.5 billion round at $11-13 billion — a reported ~160% valuation increase in under six months, not confirmed by the company.
- Pure-play MLOps tooling remains capital-starved by comparison: ZenML closed a $3.7M extension led by Point Nine in October 2023 bringing total seed to $6.4M, and ClearML shows roughly $11-16M disclosed with no credible 2025-2026 mega-round — no European MLOps tooling vendor in this window raised at a scale comparable to the compute layer.
